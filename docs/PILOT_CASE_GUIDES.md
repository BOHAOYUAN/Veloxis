# Veloxis Founding Advisor Pilot — Guided Cases

These facilitation notes accompany the two fictional households in the public demo. They are for evaluating a client-meeting conversation with synthetic data only. They are not investment, tax, legal, or financial advice.

## Case 1: Mid-career household

### Meeting setup

The fictional household is age 32 with employment income, living and housing costs, several investment tax categories, and a one-time education goal. The Current Plan retires at 60. The Proposed Plan changes retirement age, annual savings, and retirement spending.

### Conversation objective

Show how a small set of explicit changes moves the modeled result while both plans use the same simulated market paths.

### Suggested walkthrough

1. Open **Current vs Proposed** and name the four editable levers.
2. Reset Proposed to Current. Confirm that both plans produce identical results.
3. Change one lever at a time. Ask which change the evaluator expects to matter most before reading the result.
4. Open **Monte Carlo** and compare Current with Proposed using the displayed shared seed.
5. Open **Cash-flow map** and inspect the retirement-year boundary and the education-goal year.
6. End by reading the calculated difference statement. Do not turn the result into a recommendation.

### Feedback prompts

- Which part of this comparison would save time in a real meeting?
- Which number would you need to verify before showing it to a client?
- What is missing from the explanation, even if the calculation is correct?

## Case 2: Near-retirement household

### Meeting setup

The fictional household is age 57 with five years until the Current Plan retirement date, a mortgage that extends beyond that date, a one-time family-support goal, and user-provided Social Security income. The Proposed Plan delays retirement and claiming while lowering retirement spending.

### Conversation objective

Explore whether the visual explanation remains useful when the household has little time to recover from poor early retirement returns.

### Suggested walkthrough

1. Begin with **Current vs Proposed** and compare the plan-end probability and median modeled assets.
2. Reset Proposed to Current, then change retirement age only. Explain that employment income ending at the Current retirement boundary moves to the Proposed boundary.
3. Change retirement spending and annual savings separately to expose their individual modeled effects.
4. Change Social Security claim age and state the limitation: the annual estimate does not automatically change with the claiming age.
5. Use **Stress test** to discuss assumption sensitivity without calling any scenario a forecast.
6. Use **Cash-flow map** to inspect the retirement boundary, mortgage period, and family-support goal.

### Feedback prompts

- Would this help before, during, or after a client meeting?
- Does using the same paths make the comparison easier to trust or explain?
- Which missing workflow would prevent you from using this in practice?

## Facilitator boundary

- Use only the two built-in fictional households.
- Do not ask the evaluator for client inputs or documents.
- Describe results as modeled outcomes under entered assumptions.
- Record the evaluator's exact language, especially objections and current workarounds.
- Do not promise feature delivery or present Veloxis as a replacement for a planning or compliance system.
